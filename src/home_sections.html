01 Inside a sample ad

Why the $120 fee stands out.

The posting calls the fee refundable. Read the condition attached to it. The applicant has to send money to receive the tools for the job. JobShield flags this request and shows the words that triggered the warning.

Excerpt from the sample posting

Remote Data Entry Assistant

“Selected applicants must pay a refundable registration and onboarding fee of $120 via wire transfer to confirm their kit and software license.”

Upfront payment request. Find the employer’s official contact details independently and verify the opening before sending money.

02 How screening works

What the AI reads.

Paste the full advertisement, including the application instructions. Once there is enough text to analyze, JobShield checks the posting in two ways and brings the findings into one report.

Model · language patterns

Words in context

A classifier trained on labeled job ads weighs individual words and two-word phrases. The result shows which terms influenced its assessment of your posting.

Rules · specific requests

Concrete warning signs

Separate checks look for requests such as paying a training deposit, supplying banking credentials, or buying equipment from a specified vendor to qualify.

Report · supporting detail

Evidence to inspect

Read each matched phrase alongside its explanation. If the rules catch a fee request in otherwise ordinary wording, the warning still appears in the report.

04 Model evaluation

How it performed on held-out ads.

The team selected the classifier and its decision threshold using a separate validation set. On the final test, 130 of the 2,682 postings were labeled fraudulent.

101 / 130

Fraudulent ads caught

The classifier reached 77.69% recall. It missed 29 fraudulent ads in the test set.

90.18%

Precision

Of the 112 ads the classifier flagged, 101 were labeled fraudulent and 11 were legitimate.

83.47%

F1 score

This measure balances how often the classifier caught fraud with how often its flags were correct.
